### A Quiet Breakfast Interrupted By Uninvited Guests

The novel begins in a hole in the ground, which was not a nasty, dirty, wet hole, but a comfortable one belonging to Bilbo Baggins. Bilbo is a respectable hobbit whose daily schedule consists mostly of eating second breakfasts, smoking a pipe, and avoiding anything remotely adventurous.

His peaceful life is abruptly upended when an elderly wizard marks his front door with a scratched symbol, inviting thirteen hungry dwarves to eat all his food and sign him up as an official "burglar" for a dangerous mountain expedition.

Gandalf is technically in charge, though he has a habit of disappearing whenever trouble gets interesting.

---

### Plot, Under Constant Threat Of Being Eaten

The journey toward the Lonely Mountain involves a long series of terrible decisions and narrow escapes across Middle-earth:

- getting captured by three trolls who spend too much time arguing over recipes
- losing an entire dwarf company inside a goblin-infested mountain range
- a high-stakes riddle contest in a dark cave with a pale, miserable creature named Gollum
- being carried away from giant burning trees by oversized eagles
- wandering off the path in a dark forest full of giant talking spiders

At one point, Bilbo slips on a gold ring that makes him invisible, transforming him from a reluctant liability into the most essential member of the team.

---

### Participants In The Situation

The party consists of a single sensible hobbit, one wizard, and thirteen headstrong dwarves with varying degrees of competence.

- **Bilbo Baggins** – A small fellow who misses his pocket-handkerchiefs and armchair, but possesses surprising resourcefulness when cornered.
- **Gandalf** – A wizard who delivers cryptic advice, creates fireworks, and leaves the party right before they enter dangerous forests.
- **Thorin Oakenshield** – The proud leader of the dwarves, whose determination to reclaim his ancestral gold occasionally cloud his judgment.
- **Smaug** – A massive, arrogant dragon who sleeps on a pile of treasure and loves hearing compliments about his waistcoat of diamonds.

Their personal development occurs mostly while shivering in barrels, dodging arrows, and missing lunch.

---

### Topics The Book Mentions Frequently

The story explores several timeless themes wrapped in a grand fairytale:

- how ordinary, quiet people can display remarkable courage when called upon
- the corrupting influence of greed and dragon-sickness over shared treasure
- the hospitality of strange creatures vs. the hospitality of hobbit-holes
- why it is always a bad idea to give nicknames to giant spiders

A marginal note insists that pocket-handkerchiefs are an essential piece of adventuring equipment, regardless of what dwarves think.

---

### Favourite Passage

> "If more of us valued food and cheer and song above hoarded gold, it would be a merrier world."

This quiet statement delivered near the conclusion highlights the core philosophy of the entire adventure.

---

### Final Thoughts

The story concludes with a massive five-way battle, a sobering lesson about the true cost of treasure, and a very tired hobbit returning home just in time to stop people from auctioning off his furniture.

Readers may enjoy this book if they appreciate:

- cozy yet epic fairytale adventures
- witty, charming narrator commentary
- iconic creatures, magic rings, and dragon confrontations
- stories about unlikely heroes discovering their inner strength

Overall rating: **10 out of 10 shiny brass buttons**.
