### A Reality That Never Quite Existed, Yet Never Left

The novel begins with a clock striking thirteen, immediately establishing that time itself has been enlisted into the service of the state. It follows Winston Smith, an unremarkable clerk who spends his days rewriting history to match the government's latest opinions.

Winston buys an illegal paper diary, an act of defiance that begins with a single sentence and escalates into a desperate attempt to retain his own thoughts. From this moment onward, the narrative moves with the quiet dread of someone walking down a hallway where even the walls are listening.

It is abundantly clear who is in charge, and they are not open to feedback.

---

### Plot, Under Permanent Surveillance

The story revolves around Winston’s quiet rebellion against the total control of Big Brother and the Party. His attempt to preserve human feeling encounters several systemic obstacles:

- telescreens that monitor both facial expressions and breathing patterns
- a Ministry of Truth that regularly deletes people from historical records
- a language called Newspeak, systematically designed to make illegal thoughts impossible
- an underground resistance that may or may not be an elaborate trap

At one point, Winston and his lover Julia believe they have found a secret refuge, briefly forgetting that privacy is a punishable offense.

---

### Participants In The Situation

The characters operate under extreme constraint, aware that every gesture could be their last.

- **Winston Smith** – A low-ranking Party member whose main crime is possessing a memory.
- **Julia** – A practical rebel who agrees that the Party is evil, but prefers breaking small rules over saving the world.
- **O'Brien** – An enigmatic inner party official who appears to understand Winston’s secret thoughts better than Winston himself.
- **Big Brother** – A face on a poster that observes everyone, existing simultaneously everywhere and nowhere.

Their personal growth occurs mostly in windowless rooms where reality is subject to vote.

---

### Topics The Book Mentions Frequently

The novel examines several uncomfortable questions about power and truth:

- whether past events exist if no record or memory remains
- the weaponization of language to limit human thought
- the administrative efficiency of total surveillance
- the terrifying idea that $2 + 2 = 5$ if the Party demands it

A historical appendix suggests the system eventually outlived its own language, but the story itself offers no comfortable escape.

---

### Favourite Passage

> Freedom is the freedom to say that two plus two make four. If that is granted, all else follows.

This short observation serves as the central anchor of Winston’s entire struggle against reality manipulation.

---

### Final Thoughts

The ending arrives not as an explosive triumph, but as a quiet, devastating victory for the institution over the individual. 

Readers may appreciate this book if they are drawn to:

- bleak, atmospheric world-building
- psychological tension over fast-paced action
- deep explorations of political control and propaganda
- stories where the system is the ultimate antagonist

Overall rating: **9 out of 10 watchful telescreens**.
